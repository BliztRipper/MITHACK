Retina CVD/CRAO

One retinal photo, two readings.

Decision support for Thai frontline clinicians: a 10-year cardiovascular risk for screening, and an eye-stroke triage card for emergencies. Every result stays preliminary until a clinician signs it.

Siriraj x MIT Hackathon, cardiovascular track. A prototype on synthetic patients; not a medical device.

Thailand already photographs the retina at scale. Community hospitals run diabetic-retinopathy screening cameras. This tool uses retinal cameras already in diabetic-eye screening; no new camera is assumed.

Cardiovascular disease causes about 120,000 deaths a year in Thailand (GBD estimate, via the World Heart Federation). Many high-risk people are never assessed, because a full work-up needs labs and a clinician's time.

On the acute side, central retinal artery occlusion, an eye stroke, is missed by non-ophthalmologists in the hours when stroke work-up matters most.

So one capture flow serves both: Screening for the slow risk, Emergency for the fast one. The software prepares the case; a clinician decides it.

What a case returns

Screening: a risk number with its caveat

The 2013 ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations give the 10-year risk for ages 40 to 79. The 2018 AHA/ACC statin groups come first: known ASCVD, LDL of 190 mg/dL or more, or diabetes at 40 to 75 bypass the calculator.

The artery-to-vein ratio sits beside it as an investigational enhancer. It never changes the number.

Emergency: a triage card with its reasons

Fixed, unit-tested rules turn photo quality, onset time and symptoms into one of five levels and an ordered list of actions.

This build has no trained CRAO model, so the card says so and escalates every gradable case to an ophthalmologist.

The flow

First, consent is recorded.

Retinal photos are biometric health data under Thai PDPA section 26. Care use and model improvement are separate ticks, and refusing the second changes nothing about care.

Honest limits

Is it clinically validated? No. It is a hackathon prototype on synthetic Thai patients: not a medical device, not validated, not for patient care. It is decision support, not diagnosis.

Does it detect CRAO? Not yet. There is no trained CRAO model in this build. It runs with no model loaded, so there is no probability and no heatmap, and every gradable Emergency case goes to an ophthalmologist.

What would a model learn from? About 151 public images of established retinal artery occlusion (Mendeley RAO v2) plus RFMiD 1.0, both CC BY 4.0. No public hyperacute images exist, so no claim is made for the first 4.5 hours.

Are the thresholds validated? No. The probability bands, the quality cut-off, the time windows and the artery-to-vein reference interval are placeholders, and the app labels them so.

Does PCE fit Thai patients? It was derived in US populations and may overestimate risk for Thai patients; every result says so. The coefficients are not yet checked against the published paper. AHA PREVENT was considered and left for later.

Does the retina change the risk number? No. Vessel measurements are investigational enhancers for discussion. On the gradable demo images the ratio, 0.55 to 0.59, falls below the placeholder interval, so every one is flagged.

Does a normal-looking photo reassure? No. Sudden painless loss of vision in one eye within 24 hours, or with unknown onset, is escalated whatever the photo shows.

Who makes the decision? A clinician signs every result. Signing is a click, so monitoring flags agreement near 100% over 50 cases, after the fact.

Can records be altered? An IRIS superuser can edit records and the audit log, which is not tamper-evident. Demo users live in a file; there is no real identity provider.

Can it be sold? Not as built. RETFound weights are CC BY-NC 4.0, for demo and research only; a swap to FLAIR (Apache-2.0) is documented, not built. No regulatory route has been assessed.

Does a language model write anything? No. Triage is readable rules, and every explanation is a fixed template.

How fast is it? The demo cache holds 4 of 6 planned images. An uncached analysis can take more than 90 seconds.
