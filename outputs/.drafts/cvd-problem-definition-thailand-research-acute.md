## Findings

### Stroke: time to hospital
- Prehospital delay, Thai older adults, 2 northern hospitals, n=120 first AIS (Nurs Health Sci 2023, PMID 36240046): "70% of older persons experienced delays of over 3 hours following the onset of stroke symptoms." Higher perceived severity and shorter distance → less delay.
- Ban Phaeo Hospital, n=106 older AIS survivors, 2025 (Brain Behav 2026, PMID 42625133): mean prehospital delay 253.36 min (SD 278.70); "when mandatory in-hospital processing times are considered, intravenous thrombolysis would be effectively precluded for most patients arriving at this stage." Delay negatively correlated with stroke severity.
- Decision delay is a significant contributor to prehospital delay (West J Nurs Res 2023, PMID 35711105) — title-level only; country not verified in abstract.
- Rural southern Thai community survey, n=247 (BMJ Open 2024, PMID 38326263): "most high-risk participants (99/113) had a low to no chance awareness of their risk to stroke"; median warning-symptom knowledge 6/10.
- Siriraj-type academic stroke fast track (J Neuroimaging 2012, PMID 21122006; 2005–2006 data): fast track reduced time to CT (vs 2.5 h before), yet "Overall, 5.5% of ischemic stroke patients received intravenous rt-PA". Old single-centre data.
- International RES-Q registry (Int J Stroke 2026, PMID 40698920): across 70 countries, EMS arrival ranged 7–97% and IVT rates <1% to 52% — shows wide system variation (Thailand-specific values not extracted).

### Stroke: system capacity
- Thai hospital survey 2019, 38/119 hospitals responded (BMC Health Serv Res 2022, PMID 36539806): stroke units in all advanced/standard-level and 55% of mid-level referral hospitals; neurologists in 100% advanced and 50% standard-level hospitals; rehab physicians at standard/mid-level hospitals only a quarter of advanced-level. Low response rate (32%).
- Claims 2017–2020 (PMID 37280024): treatment outside Bangkok associated with increased mortality; thrombolysis associated with decreased risk of death; only 32% received rehabilitation.
- Thai national IS cohort (PMID 42504160): ≤30-day post-discharge mortality 8.2% exceeds in-hospital 6.4% → the first month after discharge is a high-risk window.
- National IVT rate for Thailand: NOT FOUND in this run (searches returned 0 or irrelevant). Mark UNVERIFIED.

### Heart attack (STEMI)
- TACSR 2002–2005, 9,373 ACS (J Med Assoc Thai 2007, PMID 18431881): only 52.6% of STEMI received reperfusion; primary PCI 22.2%; median door-to-needle 85 min, door-to-balloon 122 min; median time-to-treatment 240 min (lysis) and 359 min (PCI).
- TACSR STEMI in-hospital mortality 17% (PMID 18431891).
- TRACS 2007–2008, 2,007 ACS, 39 centres (PMID 22612004): 67% of STEMI reperfused (lysis 42.6%, PPCI 24.7%); median door-to-needle 65 min, door-to-balloon 127 min; median time-to-treatment 285 min (lysis) and 324 min (PCI); "lower in-hospital but still high mortality at one-year".
- UCS claims 2011–2017, 69,031 STEMI (BMC Cardiovasc Disord 2020, PMID 32143572): PCI recipients increased 5.7 percentage points/yr; provinces outside Bangkok with >50% PCI rose from 4 (2011) to 37 (2017); reperfusion vs none lowered 30-day mortality by 19.6 pp (PCI) and 14.1 pp (lysis only); mortality among non-reperfused patients rose 0.79 pp/yr. Thailand has 77 provinces (general knowledge; not sourced here) → ~40 provinces still below 50% PCI in 2017 (inference).
- UCS 2017 ACS cohort (Glob Health Res Policy 2024, PMID 39563455): CAG/PCI access associated with lower 5-year mortality (HR 0.55 STEMI; 0.57 NSTE-ACS) "despite the limited availability".

### Cardiac arrest
- PAROS Thailand 2017–2020, 1,240 EMS-transported OHCA, 3 centres (Resusc Plus 2022, PMID 35036967): 30-day survival 3.4%; bystander CPR 40.4%; bystander CPR with AED 0.8%; each 1-minute faster EMS response aRR 1.12 for survival; bystander CPR aRR 1.88.

### Why time matters (global)
- Saver 2006 (Stroke, PMID 16339467): in a typical large-vessel ischaemic stroke, 1.9 million neurons are lost each minute; 120 million neurons per hour.
- Emberson 2014 (Lancet, PMID 25106063), IPD of 6,756 patients: alteplase within 3 h good outcome 32.9% vs 23.1% (OR 1.75); 3–4.5 h OR 1.26; >4.5 h OR 1.15 (n.s.).

## Conflicts / gaps
- Thai ACS registry data are old (2002–2008); newer national registry delay data not found in this run.
- National Thai stroke thrombolysis rate and EMS (1669) utilisation for stroke/STEMI: not found; flagged as a data gap.
- Prehospital-delay studies are small, single-region samples.
