## Findings

### Q1. AHA 2021 scientific statement (Mac Grory et al., Stroke 2021): done. The full text was blocked (HTTP 403 for both HTML and PDF), so these points come from the abstract and the official press release.
- Abstract, verbatim: "Acute CRAO is a medical emergency. Systems of care should evolve to prioritize early recognition and triage of CRAO to emergency medical attention. There is considerable variability in management patterns… The current literature suggests that treatment with intravenous tissue plasminogen activator may be effective. Patients should undergo urgent screening and treatment of vascular risk factors. There is a need for high-quality, randomized clinical trials" [1].
- Referral: the press release quotes the statement: "if a person is diagnosed in a doctor's office or other outpatient clinic, they should be immediately sent to a hospital emergency department for further evaluation and treatment." [2]. Neither the release nor the abstract uses the phrase "stroke centre" (checked). The statement frames CRAO as "a form of acute ischemic stroke" [1].
- Time window: "tPA must be administered within 4.5 hours of the first sign of symptoms to be most effective and safe" [2]. Hyperbaric oxygen and intra-arterial alteplase "show promise but require further study" [2].
- Secondary prevention is to be "a collaborative effort between neurologists, ophthalmologists, cardiologists and primary care clinicians" [2]. The exact work-up list (MRI, vascular imaging, cardiac monitoring, ESR/CRP) could not be read from the primary source. It is UNVERIFIED from [1]; Q3 gives primary evidence for each element.
- Context, US practice survey [28]: only 20% of 45 institutions had a formal CRAO policy. The primary treating physician was an ophthalmologist in 44% and a neurologist in 27%. Ocular massage was offered by 66%, paracentesis by 42%, and IVT by 53%. "many programs do not routinely refer patients to an emergency department."

### Q2. Thrombolysis evidence: done
**RCTs (all ≤4.5 h, all against aspirin; none positive):**
- **THEIA** (France, 16 stroke-unit hospitals, phase 3, double-dummy) [3]. 70 randomised from 2018 to 2023, Snellen <20/400, alteplase 0.9 mg/kg vs aspirin 300 mg. Mean onset-to-treatment was 232.4 min. Primary outcome (≥0.3 logMAR gain at 1 month): **19/29 (66%) with alteplase vs 13/27 (48%) with aspirin**; risk difference 17.4 (95% CI −11.8 to 46.5); aOR 1.1 (0.07–18.39); p=0.95. There was 1 asymptomatic ICH and no symptomatic haemorrhage. The authors call the trial "likely underpowered".
- **TenCRAOS** (16 sites, 6 countries, phase 3, double-blind) [4]. 78 randomised, tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg vs aspirin 300 mg. Primary outcome was BCVA ≤0.7 logMAR (≥20/100) at 30 days: **8/40 (20%) vs 9/38 (24%)**; risk difference −3.7 pp (95% CI −22.0 to 14.7); P=0.69. More adverse events with tenecteplase, **including one fatal ICH**. The authors conclude it was "associated with serious safety concerns." Per [9], 69% of TenCRAOS patients were treated within 3 h, against 12% in THEIA.
- **REVISION** (Germany; NCT04965038) [5]. Inclusion was CRAO **≤12 h** from onset, confirmed by an ophthalmologist. The CT.gov intervention reads "Tenecteplase (until trial protocol V04: Alteplase)" vs placebo. Status is **TERMINATED, "Stopped after prespecified interim analysis due to futility"**, with actual enrolment 127, completion 2026-04-29 and last update 2026-05-18. No results are posted, and no peer-reviewed results paper was found as of 2026-10-17. The originally planned sample size of 422 comes from a secondary snippet (First10EM) and is UNVERIFIED.
- Trial authors (Lancet Neurol 2026 reply [9]): "current evidence does not indicate that shorter treatment delays translate into improved visual outcomes following thrombolysis". They report 3 ICH in the thrombolysis groups across the two trials (1 fatal) and none with aspirin. An IPD meta-analysis of THEIA, TenCRAOS and REVISION is planned.

**Observational meta-analyses (positive signal, before the RCTs):**
- Mac Grory 2020 [8]: in the cohort, 44% recovered with alteplase <4.5 h vs 13.1% untreated. The updated patient-level MA (238 pts) gave 37.3% recovery with alteplase <4.5 h vs 17.7% without treatment.
- Shahjouei 2024 [7]: 771 pts from 72 publications. IVT-tPA <4.5 h: 74.3% improved ≥0.3 logMAR and 39.0% reached VA ≥20/100. IAT <24 h: 60.0% and 21.9%.
- Xie 2025 IPD MA [6]: 783 pts. IVT <4.5 h recovery from severe vision loss **28.8% vs 11.1%** (OR 3.32, 1.24–8.92). IAT <6 h: 27.2% vs 12.0% (OR 2.72). IAT shows a changepoint at 8 h. The authors estimate an RCT needs 95 per arm for 80% power at OR 3.0. Both completed RCTs were smaller than this.
- Real-world, non-randomised: Kaiser NorCal's telemedicine fundus-photo pathway with tenecteplase [33], n=85. ≥0.3 logMAR improvement in 54.3% treated vs 28% observed (p=.014); 2 ICH.

**Guidelines (they conflict, and the dates matter):**
- **ESO 2025** (published 22 May 2025, before both RCTs were published) [10]. The full-text PICO 9 reads: "In adults with ocular stroke (central retinal artery occlusion), we **suggest** thrombolysis within 4.5 h of stroke onset… Quality of evidence: Very low. Strength of recommendation: Weak for intervention". The abstract uses "We **recommend** thrombolysis within 4.5 h". The document is internally inconsistent.
- **AHA/ASA 2026 AIS guideline** (updated 26 Jan 2026) [11], as quoted by Guideline Central: "In adults with acute nonarteritic CRAO causing disabling visual loss, and who are otherwise eligible for IVT, the usefulness of treatment with IVT within 4.5 hours of time last known well is uncertain. (2b, C-LD)". The primary was blocked (403), so the COR/LOE could not be confirmed directly. This is a secondary quote.
- Poor natural history and limited benefit of conservative care: in the Kaiser NorCal cohort (n=794), 92.6% of 217 patients who presented within 4.5 h and had follow-up did not improve beyond 20/200. Conservative treatment made no difference [51]. The AHA puts the share regaining functional vision at <20% [2].

### Q3. Stroke/MI risk, silent infarcts, work-up: done
- **Timing**: Korean national self-controlled case series (n=1,655 incident CRAO) [12]. Stroke/AMI IRR was 14.0 for days 1–30 and **peaked at days 1–7 (IRR 44.51, 95% CI 27.07–73.20)**. Risk was also raised 1–30 days *before* CRAO (IRR 6.82).
- **Absolute risk is lower in population data (conflict)**: Olmsted County, 1976–2016 (n=89) [13] had symptomatic ischemic stroke in **2.2%** within ±15 days. The authors call this "slightly lower than most studies from tertiary centers". In a Mayo cohort (n=300) [14], 5.3% had symptomatic stroke around the event (2.3% before, 1.3% simultaneous, 1.7% after), and 7% of embolic CRAO did.
- Meta-analysis (Thai authors) [20]: ischemic CVD in 14.4% after CRAO (95% CI 11.4–18.0) and 22.2% after BRAO. Yet the same abstract reports CRAO OR 1.59 vs BRAO, which is **internally inconsistent**. RAO vs non-RAO OR 2.75. A 2026 Turkish cohort found MACCE in 13.7% over a median 26.5 months, with 26.9% of events in month 1 (PMID 42627251, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42627251/).
- **Silent/acute brain infarcts on DWI**: 24.2% of RAO, 37.5% of them asymptomatic (n=33) [15]. Silent infarcts in 15.1% of retinal ischemia (n=112) [16]. Acute ischemic stroke in 32.5% (25/77) of MRI-scanned CRAO in Mannheim [17]. 18% (17/94) had concurrent cerebral infarcts in the Emory 2023–26 cohort [31]. Olmsted: 1/15 (6.7%) asymptomatic diffusion restriction [13]. The range is roughly 7–32%, depending on setting and selection.
- **Work-up yield**:
  - Callizo prospective study [18]: ≥1 new vascular risk factor in 78%, ipsilateral carotid stenosis ≥70% in 40%, AF in 20%, new hypertension in 16%.
  - New AF detected in 49.6% at 2 years after CRAO (n=100), HR 1.64 vs matched controls. The authors advise long-term cardiac monitoring [19].
  - Emory aetiology [31]: ICA stenosis 32%, cardiac source 21%, aortic arch atheroma 6%, GCA 5%, hypercoagulable 6%, none found 28%.
  - Hayreh [34]: carotid plaque in 67% of those tested, >50% stenosis in 32%.
- **GCA labs**: see Q6. THEIA excluded patients with clinical or laboratory (raised CRP) evidence of GCA (CT.gov eligibility, NCT03197194).

### Q4. Diagnostic delay and tele-models: done
- **Delay**:
  - Swiss Stroke Registry, 2014–2019, 397 CRAO vs 32,816 strokes [25]: only **25.6% of CRAO arrived <4 h**. Symptom-to-door was **852 min vs 300 min** for stroke. Lysis or thrombectomy was given in 13.2% vs 30.9%. Only 28.6% of the public recognised CRAO symptoms, and 55.4% of those would go directly to an ED (90.0% for stroke).
  - Mannheim [17]: ~60% presented beyond 4.5 h. Prehospital delay was the main reason for no IVT (58.8%). 44.4% saw a private ophthalmologist first.
  - Emory 2021 [27]: 34% presented <24 h, and only 3/181 got IVT despite a protocol. Emory 2026 [31]: median onset-to-presentation 17.35 h, with only 16% of eyes <4.5 h.
  - Mean onset-to-presentation 31 ± 65 h in an older series (n=31) [PMID 19374663, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19374663/].
- **Ophthalmologist access**: Shah 2023 (n=484) [26]. 51% contacted the health system within 4.5 h, but **only 81/247 (32.8%) of those saw an ophthalmologist within 4.5 h**. 60.3% first presented to an ED and 27.5% to an ophthalmologist. 35.5% did not see an ophthalmologist within 24 h. Black and Hispanic patients presented later (40.4 vs 23.0 h). The base paper puts initial ophthalmologist evaluation at "<40%" [52].
- **Non-ophthalmic physician skill**: FOTO-ED phase II [30]. Emergency physicians reading non-mydriatic photos found relevant findings with **sensitivity 46%** (95% CI 29–63) and specificity 91%. In the base paper, three stroke neurologists reached sensitivity 50–63% for hyperacute CRAO [52]. Biousse's Merritt lecture says ophthalmoscopy by non-ophthalmologists is "infrequently and poorly performed" [PMID 29273687, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29273687/].
- **Fundus may look near-normal early**:
  - Hayreh [34]: cherry-red spot 90% and posterior-pole retinal opacity 58% at first visit, but "CRAO associated with normal-appearing retinal vessels and/or optic disk is not rare."
  - Emory 2024 [32]: of 9 eyes imaged <12 h, only 5 had (subtle) whitening on colour photos, but all had OCT inner-retinal hyper-reflectivity.
  - **Emory 2026 [31], <4.5 h group: colour fundus photo diagnosed 68.7% (11/16) vs OCT 93.7%**. Overall, 19% of eyes were diagnosed only on OCT. Colour-photo quality was adequate in only 61% (OCT 70%).
  - Retinal DWI on MRI is no shortcut: 39.3% detection when <12 h [50].
- **Tele-models**:
  - **FOTO-ED** (Emory) [29]: nurse practitioners took non-mydriatic photos. 83% of patients had ≥1 high-quality eye, and the median session was 1.9 min.
  - **Emory Eye-Stroke protocol** (Topcon Maestro-2 hybrid fundus+OCT in the ED, linked to Epic; remote on-call ophthalmologist) [31]. For <4.5 h patients, door-to-imaging was **28.5 min vs 3.025 h** pre-protocol and door-to-needle was 58 min. **IVT 7/16 (43.7%) vs 2/12 (16.6%)** before, with no thrombolysis adverse events. Failures: 1 camera malfunction and 1 remote-reader miss of subtle OCT signs.
  - **Kaiser Permanente NorCal** telemedicine fundus-photo pathway (ophthalmology + EM + stroke, tenecteplase): 85 patients <4.5 h from 2021 to 2023 [33].
  - Buffalo (Lancet Neurol 2026 letter, PMID 42009000) describes a "streamlined central retinal artery occlusion pathway" and blames system-of-care gaps for THEIA's result. Only a snippet was read, so the details are UNVERIFIED.
  - Mannheim [17]: after an in-hospital guideline, primary stroke-unit admission rose from 52.2% to 97.4% and the IVT rate from 0% to 14.1%.

### Q5. Incidence, Thai data, Thai stroke fast track, ophthalmologist distribution: done. There are gaps.
- **Incidence**:
  - Olmsted County: 1.33/100k crude and **1.90/100k** age/sex-adjusted [21]. Later Olmsted, 1976–2016: 2.58/100k [13].
  - Korea: **1.80/100k PY** for 2008–2011 [22]; 2.00/100k PY standardized for 2002–2015, falling over time [23]; **2.25/100k PY** for 2005–2018 [24]. Incidence peaks at age 80–84 at 10.08/100k [22]. CRAO SMR is 9.95 [24].
- **Thai CRAO incidence: not found** (searched PubMed "retinal artery occlusion[ti] Thailand" and the web). UNVERIFIED. Treat any Thai incidence as an extrapolation from Korean figures (inference only).
- **Thai CRAO care evidence found**: all single-centre, small and retrospective. Siriraj HBOT cohort (2003–2022; n=17 HBOT vs 32 control; rt-PA patients excluded) [48]. Ramathibodi intra-ophthalmic alteplase+nimodipine (n=9) [49]. Chulalongkorn meta-analysis of stroke risk [20]. No Thai IVT-for-CRAO series was found.
- **Thai stroke fast track**:
  - NHSO started free IV thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke in 2008, initially at university hospitals in Greater Bangkok, then nationwide, with a 270-minute window [47].
  - NHSO has paid an add-on of **49,000 THB per rt-PA case since FY2551 (2008)**. The ischemic stroke dataset is defined by principal diagnosis **I63.0–I63.9** [46].
  - NHSO counted 112,776 ischemic strokes in FY2567 (2024) [46]. The extracted rt-PA rate rose "from 0.53% (2562) to 8.46% (2566)" [46]. The 0.53% figure looks implausibly low and may be an extraction error; verify it against the original chart.
  - **Is CRAO included?** The NHSO document does not mention H34 (retinal artery occlusion) or eye stroke [46]. No Thai source was found that includes CRAO in the stroke fast track. UNVERIFIED, flagged as a gap.
- **Ophthalmologists in Thailand**:
  - 2008: **1.52/100k** (residents weighted 0.5). **45.7% in Bangkok**, which holds 10.5% of the population. Provincial ratio ranges 0.12–5.96 (median 0.58), Gini 0.815. **56/76 provinces (73.7%) are below the WHO ≥1/100k target**. 82.7% of districts, holding 63.8% of the population, have no ophthalmologist [44].
  - National workforce database: **0.8/100k (2015) to 1.7/100k (2024)**, provincial Gini 0.50 to 0.42, classed as a "scarce" specialty [45].
  - Conflict: 1.52 (2008) vs 0.8 (2015) reflects different sources and denominators (Royal College directory vs MoPH workforce database with imputation). Do not compare them as a trend.

### Q6. GCA as a must-not-miss cause: done
- Hayreh classifies CRAO into 4 entities, one of them **arteritic CRAO from GCA**. "Arteritic CRAO shows no change" in vision [36].
- Share of CRAO that is arteritic: **"<5%"** (SVIN review [38]) and **"4.5%"** (Moran CORE citing Varma 2013 [37]). The Varma abstract (PMID 23470793) does not contain the 4.5% figure, so it is unverified at the primary level. The Emory 2023–26 cohort found GCA as the cause in 5% [31].
- Of GCA patients, 1.6% develop CRAO (population-based, Olmsted). 20% of those with permanent visual loss had **no systemic symptoms** [39].
- Pathway handling:
  - Thrombolysis trials exclude GCA. THEIA excluded "headache with jaw claudication or scalp tenderness, no temporal pulse" or "elevated CRP" (CT.gov NCT03197194).
  - EyeWiki, citing the AAO PPP as a strong recommendation, advises ESR, CRP and CBC with platelets in patients over 50 with GCA symptoms, immediate corticosteroids, and temporal artery biopsy within 2 weeks of starting steroids (https://eyewiki.org/Central_Retinal_Artery_Occlusion; secondary).
  - Real-world testing is poor. At Kentucky, only 12.2% of ED CRAO patients had temporal artery ultrasound. In Epic Cosmos, 62.8% of 17,372 at-risk patients had no GCA testing [40].
- **Asia/Thailand**: GCA is ~20× less frequent in Asians at UCSF (n=38, OR 0.049) [41], and "very rare among Asians" (6 Thai biopsy-proven cases) [42]. Pooled GCA incidence is 10.00/100k aged >50, with no pooled Asian estimate in the abstract [43]. **No Thai figure for arteritic CRAO was found** (UNVERIFIED).

## Implications for the enhanced version / prototype (INFERENCE: not stated by the sources)
1. **Reframe the value proposition from "enable thrombolysis" to "triage to a stroke work-up plus a thrombolysis-trial/guideline decision".** Two phase-3 RCTs within 4.5 h were null [3][4], REVISION stopped for futility [5], and AHA 2026 rates IVT as "uncertain" (2b) [11]. The defensible clinical benefit is the 7-day stroke/MI peak (IRR 44.5) [12], the new AF found in ~50% [19], ≥70% carotid stenosis in up to 40% [18], and the 15–32% rate of acute or silent infarcts [15][16][17]. The base paper's own framing is "improving patient selection for fibrinolytic trials and secondary stroke prevention" [52].
2. **Output a pathway card, not just a class label.** On a CRAO-positive result, the prototype should show:
   - (a) "same-day ED / stroke-unit referral". Thailand's fast track is coded around I63 [46], so the card should say "activate stroke team, CRAO ≈ ocular ischaemic stroke".
   - (b) a work-up checklist: brain MRI-DWI, carotid imaging, ECG plus prolonged AF monitoring, echo, lipids/HbA1c.
   - (c) a **GCA red-flag gate**: age >50 plus jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, PMR symptoms, or raised ESR/CRP gives "urgent steroids/GCA pathway; not a lysis candidate". This matters despite rare GCA in Thais [41][42], because arteritic CRAO is <5% but threatens the fellow eye [36][38].
   - (d) time since onset vs 4.5 h, displayed as information with "uncertain benefit (AHA 2026 2b)" [11] and "ESO 2025 weak/very-low" [10].
3. **Add a quality gate and an "indeterminate: get OCT/ophthalmology" output.** Colour fundus photos detected only 68.7% of <4.5 h retinal ischaemia vs 93.7% for OCT [31], 39% of ED colour photos were inadequate [31], and the base model was trained on 24 h–30 d CRAO photos [52]. A negative AI result in a patient with sudden painless monocular loss must not reassure.
4. **Benchmark against the right human comparator.** ED physicians reading photos reached sensitivity 46% / specificity 91% [30] and stroke neurologists 50–63% [52]. A prototype demo can compare the model with these published figures, with the caveat that cohorts differ.
5. **Thai deployment logic.** Ophthalmologists are scarce and concentrated in Bangkok (1.7/100k in 2024 [45]; 45.7% in Bangkok in 2008 [44]), while thrombolysis-capable stroke hospitals exist nationally [46][47]. A plausible product therefore sits at the stroke-fast-track ED, with a non-mydriatic camera, AI triage and remote ophthalmologist confirmation (the Emory and Kaiser models [31][33]). Siriraj already has an acute CRAO cohort (HBOT series, 2003–2022) [48] that could serve as local validation data, subject to IRB and data access.
6. **Equity / awareness module.** Public recognition is 28.6% [25] and Black/Hispanic patients present later [26]. A FAST-style "sudden one-eye vision loss = stroke" message could be a low-cost add-on. This is UNVERIFIED as a Thai need; no Thai awareness data were found.

## Conflicts and gaps
- **Thrombolysis efficacy.** Observational IPD MAs are positive: 28.8% vs 11.1% [6], 37.3% vs 17.7% [8], 74.3% improved [7]. Both RCTs are null [3][4] and REVISION stopped for futility [5]. The outcome definitions differ: ≥0.3 logMAR gain [3][7] vs BCVA ≤0.7 logMAR [4][6]. Do not pool these numbers in a pitch.
- **Guidelines.** ESO 2025 says "suggest/recommend" with very low quality and a weak recommendation; its abstract and full text disagree [10]. AHA 2026 says "uncertain", 2b [11]. ESO predates THEIA (Nov 2025) and TenCRAOS (Jan 2026). Whether AHA 2026 accounted for TenCRAOS is UNVERIFIED (the guideline was dated 26 Jan 2026; TenCRAOS NEJM was 29 Jan 2026).
- **Stroke risk magnitude.** Tertiary/claims data show a high early relative risk [12], while population data show 2.2% absolute risk in ±15 days [13]. Both can hold, because one is relative and the other absolute risk.
- **Meta-analysis inconsistency.** [20] reports ischemic CVD at 14.4% for CRAO vs 22.2% for BRAO, yet gives CRAO OR 1.59 vs BRAO.
- **Ophthalmologist density.** 1.52/100k (2008) [44] vs 0.8/100k (2015) [45] come from different methods.
- **NHSO rt-PA rate.** "0.53% (2562)" looks implausible and may be an extraction artefact [46].
- **Gaps (UNVERIFIED / not found):** Thai CRAO incidence; whether CRAO (H34.1) is eligible for NHSO stroke fast-track reimbursement or rt-PA payment; Thai arteritic CRAO share; REVISION results; the AHA 2021 statement's exact work-up recommendations (full text blocked); the Lancet Neurol 2026 Buffalo pathway details; whether CRAO-specific ED misdiagnosis rates exist (the closest evidence is [26][30][31]).
- **Same cohort.** [26] and [51] share first author Shah R and a 484-patient subset, so they are probably the same Northern California cohort (inference). Do not count them as independent.
